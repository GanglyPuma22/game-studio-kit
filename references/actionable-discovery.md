# Actionable discovery

For an exploration encounter, state the visible invitation, available action, inference and consequence. Discovery becomes actionable when observing something changes what the player tries, predicts or can do. A scenic object alone may be valuable atmosphere; do not claim it teaches an interaction without player evidence.

Original example: a dim beacon pulses after nearby stones resonate. Let a first-time player notice the relation, try one stone, hear a changed pulse and choose whether to continue. Test whether the player can describe that causal link without a companion explaining it first. Record observation separately from the designer's interpretation; change one unclear cue and replay. This is a worked design option, not mandatory lore or implementation.

Name the question a small playable slice resolves and retain successful choices while correcting defects. Do not expand a world simulation or quest framework until a concrete encounter needs it.

Design basis: [Alex Beachum / Mobius Digital, The Intentionality of Wandering](https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/news/the-intentionality-of-wandering). The original example above is a proposed application of that exploration principle, not measured validation of this kit.
