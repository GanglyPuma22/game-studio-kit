# Lessons log (newest first)

Measured on one 80 s animated short for a game (Nov 2026), on Kling 4 and Seedance 2.5 through fal and Runway, with layouts from a game engine. Each entry says what was tried, its cost, and what changed in the method. These models change fast, so re-test anything older than a few months.

## 2026-11-10/11: frame review and key moments
- Kling 4 on fal takes only first and last frames, so a key moment (the giant's first stomp) must be a join frame between two pieces. Pieces are cut at key events.
- A frame review page where the owner circles problems and notes each frame caught issues before any spend: a weapon pointing backwards (fixed by marking its business end in the guide), a character position mismatch, a layout bug.
- The owner noticed late that one character's film design no longer matched its new canonical model. That un-approved every shot showing it. Check sheets against the current canonical model before each paid batch.

## 2026-11-09: guide A/B on a flown move
- On a 4 s FPV move with the same frames, sheets and style frame: frames only ($0.63) failed and held still after 1.9 s. Kling 4 + camera-only blob guide ($0.75) followed the path. Seedance 2.5 + guide ($2.27) was about the same. Kling 4 + guide became the default for camera-heavy shots, and a retake was approved.
- A blob near the ground read as a person standing, so a flier ran instead of flew. Fliers must read as airborne in the guide.

## 2026-11-09: flown cameras and fewer cuts (free)
- Drawn camera moves were re-flown by simulated rigs within real limits. One 82 m reveal with a 33 m drop needed 8.2 s or more, and the story gave it 4.2 s. The story was held 4.5 s instead of speeding the move up.
- 16 of 23 shots were flown, two fast-cut runs merged into single moves, and cuts fell from about 37 to about 28. The owner called it "a noticeable step up".
- Faults fixed before showing: too much FPV roll, flown cameras drifting off the action (fixed with a leash), cameras inside walls (fixed with a guard), a swoop through a large character.

## 2026-11-09: the grab froze from our inputs
- The two frames came from different sessions and showed the reverse action, the hand was metres away, the prompt forbade contact, and the closing sat on a cut. This led to the shot recipe: spec, pre-run check, shot card. The retake passed.

## 2026-11-08: single passes fail on action
- A 24 s single pass ($4.51) had the best look so far, but duplicated a character, reordered beats and parked on untimed middle frames. Two independent reviews agreed on going shot by shot.
- A three-shot test ($1.41) had one pass, one half and one fail. The rule since: the first and last frames differ by exactly one clear action.

## 2026-11-07: identity
- Inputs that disagreed (concept, boards, prompt) gave three versions of one character in one film. One sheet per character fixed it, and was on-model in 3 of 3 takes ($1.72). A sheet with four bodies gave a duplicate. A style clip did nothing.

## 2026-11-02 to 11-06: the chunk era
- Multi-shot 4-9 s chunks with a reference video (Seedance 2.5). The reference copies everything: pauses, stiff gait, sinking feet. Camera-only pillars kept the camera and freed the bodies, but swapped identities after cuts.
- Cuts within 0.75 s of a clip edge were dropped. Small fast figures in wide frames don't read: frame the hero at a quarter to a third of frame height.
- A reference image of a character held by another was blocked by moderation, whatever the text said. Stray lines were removed for free with masks and a nearby-frame fill. An extra hand at a contact beat needs a re-render with pinned limbs.
- A "game cinematic" prompt got the game's own render back. A stylized 3D feature look beat live-action on character adherence.
