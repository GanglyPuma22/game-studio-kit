# Story and purpose

## Decide what the film is for

A cinematic short made for attention is not a gameplay trailer. Studios make hype shorts before launch (Overwatch's and Clash of Clans' animated shorts are the reference class) because a good short gets watched and shared for its own sake, and that brings people to the product. So:

- **Character and ability recognition is strong:** someone who knows the product should recognise every character and what each ability does.
- **Literal accuracy is weak:** the film doesn't have to show how a real match plays. The art style may differ from the product's own render.
- **Plan the distribution with the story:** the full film goes to long-form (YouTube). Two or three high-action moments that read in under 10 s become vertical cutdowns (Reels, Shorts), each pointing to the full film and the store page. Frame those moments so they survive a 9:16 crop.

## Beats

- Show the signature verb early. A film about a grapple game that never fires a grapple has missed its point; plan where each ability appears.
- Give every character a reason for what it does next. An enemy that flees and never returns reads as a loose end.
- Length follows the story. Fill time with action and character, never with holds; the weak stretch is usually the back half, so test it hardest.
- Make a timed animatic with scratch sound before anything paid, and check comprehension: can a newcomer say why each beat happens?
- End on a title card. Keep it name-neutral until the name is chosen.
- Lock spoken lines first, and only the people who own the canon approve them.

## Sound

Clips are silent, and sound is built in the edit. Each cut's sound starts a few frames early or runs a few frames late. Music comes after picture lock, with hits synced to the cut. Use generated music or licence-checked free tracks, and record each licence. Keep character voices consistent across the film: reuse recorded lines where they fit, and convert generated speech into the canonical voice when a face must lip-sync.

## IP hygiene

Keep any inspiring show's names and terms out of prompts and copy, and show only the product's own designs.
